# Distinct information, limited prediction: Quaternary climate and species-general phylogeographic structure

**Status:** Post-result, read-only synthesis of completed frozen studies (12 November 2026). This is **not** a new confirmatory analysis, a genetic-response re-opening, a pooled meta-analysis, or a scientific amendment to the separately frozen TTF-Q manuscript. Values and Git blob identities are machine-audited in `cross_program_finite_evidence_v01.json`.

## Executive interpretation

Historical climatic displacements contain information about *relations between species* that is not reducible to modern climatic similarity, shared source/target identity or the specified geographic co-opportunity. However, three separately source-qualified, species-heldout genetic targets—population heterozygosity contrasts, pairwise Fst magnitude and mapped within-species Pleistocene break locations—did not obtain a qualified improvement from a **single LGM-to-present climatic displacement-magnitude term** beyond their respective baselines. The 211-species COI transfer test separately failed to detect reusable post-IBD spatial structure; it did not itself evaluate the LGM-term added predictive value. These are different questions, not inconsistent outcomes.

The most defensible macroecological statement is **a demonstrated separation between environmentally nonredundant history and the portability of a chosen history predictor into species-general genetic prediction**. This is an information/prediction distinction; it is not evidence against historical climate causation in any particular lineage.

## Results

### 1. Quaternary history retains distinct response-blind relational structure

The response-blind TTF-Q climate application covered 653 animal species, with 15,625 directed development dyads and 40,602 confirmatory dyads. After source/target fixed effects, contemporary climate relation, lineage and sampling imbalance, the historical-displacement relation retained 0.3647 and 0.3813 of its original standardized squared variation in development and confirmatory geometry, respectively. Adding the predeclared continuous measure of geographic co-opportunity reduced these *predictor-information survival fractions* to 0.2989 and 0.3163. The retention ratios were 0.8197 and 0.8296.

These proportions are **neither biological-response variance explained nor genetic R²**. They establish only that the historical climate relation could not be reproduced algebraically by the particular already-declared controls. They do not determine the sign or magnitude of any genetic association.

The 21-species Palearctic non-Lepidoptera application separately stopped before biological response opening when relation information and endpoint concentration failed its predeclared gate. That is an evaluability limit, not an empirical ecological null.

The mammal source contained 10,013 samples of mapped genetic-break lines and 66,687 controlled survey-background site records. All original and 50-km-jittered climate/GIS predictors passed exact source and numeric SHA checks before the single authorized outcome opening. The baseline and augmented heldout AUCs were 0.58654 and 0.58700 at the original case locations and 0.57649 and 0.57687 after jitter, respectively. The frozen history-predictor residual fraction on its training geometry was 0.6512 despite the negligible added predictive discrimination. Thus, failure to improve heldout AUC in that design cannot be attributed to the historical term having zero *observable residual covariate variation*.

The He and Fst targets have different outcome scales and different nuisance designs from break-site AUC. A shared direction of **non-qualification** should not be mistaken for a shared estimated genetic coefficient. Nor are the source studies guaranteed independent at the publication/species level: GenDivRange and CaliPopGen may overlap underlying literature, and such overlap has not been fully resolved.

### 3. A geographically stable historical predictor is not automatically a stable biological predictor

In an independently computed **response-blind** spatial-sensitivity audit of 10,013 case sites for 80 mammals, a single fixed <=50-km perturbation of each original case position yielded pooled Spearman correlation 0.9667 in the four-variable LGM-to-present climate-displacement magnitude. The median of 80 within-species Spearman correlations was 0.8470 (descriptive species-bootstrap interval approximately 0.787–0.868). This is reassuring about the *climatic covariate's* large-scale spatial ranking, not about species-general genetic prediction. Twelve species had within-species correlations below 0.5, indicating heterogeneity that pooled site-level correlation conceals.

A matched botanical negative control in the separate exploratory host–insect analysis likewise prevents overinterpretation: host-climate historical information was not consistently more distinctive for the recorded host taxa than for modern-climate-matched alternative plant taxa. Thus one cannot infer a host-specific historical corridor from predictor information survival alone.

## Discussion: what does the contrast mean?

**First, ecological history is not exhausted by contemporary climate.** Two species can have similar modern climatic environments and geographic opportunities but different contrasts between glacial and modern local climates. The TTF-Q decomposition measures precisely the additional relation geometry that remains after nuisance residualization. Its consistency across two declared dyadic panels is a valid and reproducible positive ecological-predictor result.

**Second, a measurable ecological predictor need not be an effective cross-lineage genetic predictor.** Genetic patterns are outcomes of population persistence, dispersal, colonization sequence, drift, local habitat continuity and lineage-specific responses to physical barriers. The chosen historical predictor measures local four-variable change *at present observed locations* rather than the historical routes, refugial occupancies or demographic sequences experienced by each lineage. The divergence between information and heldout genetic gain is therefore compatible with multiple genuine glacial effects that the frozen scalar does not encode. It does not identify which mechanism is responsible.

**Third, shared geographic opportunity is a limited surrogate for a shared effective landscape.** The original 211-species COI result did not detect cross-lineage post-IBD structural transfer (T ≈ 0.03257, p ≈ 0.7393) despite a separate calibrated within-species structure result (null-based p ≈ 0.00899; the raw self statistic itself was negative). That finding concerns general spatial transfer, not the added LGM term. It is consistent with, but does not prove, the possibility that the same landscape is genetically translated differently by different lineages.

**Fourth, the scientific advance should not be rescued through another response-dependent transformation.** Once He, Fst and break-location tests have been opened under their finite contracts, experimenting on those outcomes with alternative LGM epochs, signed climate vectors, refugium maps, spatial path costs, life-history strata or different geographic thresholds would be exploratory and could not be called a fresh confirmation. Testing such biologically richer history models would require a genuinely new response-disjoint design, frozen before opening its genetic responses.

## Material limitations

1. **Contemporary coordinates are not palaeo-occurrences.** CHELSA 21-ka climate queried at modern collection sites is a historical environmental contrast for those locations; it is not a reconstructed historical occupied range, migration corridor or observed isolation event.
2. **No universal genetic null is established.** Non-qualification is conditional on chosen genetic responses, source domains, one-term historical magnitude, nuisance sets, heldout evaluation and finite uncertainty. A true effect can be absent, smaller than test sensitivity, nonlinear or encoded by different historical variables.
3. **Geographical controls are incomplete surrogates.** The dyadic 500-km co-opportunity control does not capture all topographic or demographic barriers. The Jensen mammal model used source-reconstructed GIS barriers, not the original author's unpublished fitted environmental CSVs. Its break-line-background negatives represent sampled spatial availability rather than confirmed absence of breaks.
4. **Sampling, scale and study independence are constrained.** He spans many taxa and studies; Fst is California-specific with substantial unknown coordinate uncertainty; mammal break locations come from published mapped line-and-range datasets. Study overlap was not fully audited. The 50-km jitter sensitivity perturbs case locations while holding background fixed, so changes in absolute AUC need not isolate effects of one climate predictor.
5. **The three increment metrics cannot be pooled.** He and Fst use relative MSE gain; mammal break detection uses AUC difference. The original COI transfer test uses a different correlation-based statistic; source counts are not additive evidence of statistical independence.

## Inference boundary and manuscript routing

**Safe synthesis sentence:** “Late-Quaternary climate displacement retains independently measurable relational variation beyond contemporary climate and specified geographic opportunities, but the tested single-term historical predictor did not yield qualified additional out-of-species prediction of population He contrasts, Fst or mapped mammalian phylogeographic break locations.”

**Not safe:** “Climate history causes no genetic divergence,” “glacial refugia are disproven,” “82–83% of genetic variation is historical,” or “three fully independent datasets prove a genetic null.”

The pre-existing TTF-Q Methods in Ecology and Evolution manuscript is scientifically frozen and should not silently acquire these later post-outcome results. Its next blockers remain an author-selected open-source license and completed title-page declarations. This document is a separate post-result comparative synthesis/interpretive handoff on draft PR #153, **not** a revision of that frozen methods article. PRs #151 and #152 and the original TTF/COI endpoints remain closed.
