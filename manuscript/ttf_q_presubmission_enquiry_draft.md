# Presubmission enquiry draft — Methods in Ecology and Evolution

Recipient: coordinator@methodsinecologyandevolution.org

Subject: Presubmission enquiry — evaluable envelopes for dyadic ecological predictors

Dear Methods in Ecology and Evolution Editorial Office,

We are preparing a Research Article that introduces TTF-Q, a response-blind method for characterizing whether a dyadic ecological predictor can support a planned inferential claim before the biological response is opened.

TTF-Q is not a workflow that simply chains existing diagnostics. Its new inferential object is a **response-blind evaluable envelope for dyadic predictors**: an exact nested decomposition of how much focal relation geometry remains identifiable after endpoint identity and declared controls, paired with endpoint-support concentration and estimator-specific calibration/detectability. This makes a prospective claim possible that the component tools alone do not provide: before the outcome is opened, a planned dyadic effect is either licensed only within an explicit inferential domain or declined without converting that decision into a biological null.

We validate the method with pre-result-frozen known-truth simulations. Across 243 constructed dyadic designs, prescribed endpoint, nuisance-control and geographic information-survival fractions were recovered to floating-point precision. A second benchmark shows that four designs receiving the same negative single-cell qualification can represent different states: reduced identifiable information, nuisance redundancy, or anti-conservative inference caused by concentrated endpoint signal. We then apply the framework to response-blind present and late-Quaternary climate relations across 653 animal species. Finally, in a separately frozen Palearctic insect application, TTF-Q prospectively stopped a planned LGM–genetic analysis before the subgroup biological response was opened because the focal relation retained only 13.1% unique information and its residual signal exceeded the predeclared endpoint-concentration ceiling.

The statistical ingredients used to estimate individual axes are established, but the estimand, nested information-survival identities, evaluable-envelope object and prospective outcome-opening rule are new. We therefore position the contribution as a general method for deciding what a relational ecological design can support before response inspection, rather than as a software workflow. We would appreciate guidance on whether this is within scope as a Research Article before formal submission.

The manuscript is currently approximately 6,350 words excluding final title-page material, with reproducible simulations, two response-blind ecological applications, tests, and anonymized code prepared for peer review.
